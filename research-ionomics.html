Plant nutrition

Understanding plant adaptation to challenging soil environments

I investigate how plants adapt to different soil environments, with a particular focus on nutrient homeostasis. By combining digital phenotyping, ICP-MS ionomics, genetic mapping, and environmental association analyses, I aim to understand the genetic and environmental factors underlying natural variation in plant nutrient accumulation.

Digital phenotyping

Quantifying leaf chlorosis

Mineral nutrient imbalances can cause chlorosis, negatively affecting plant health and ultimately reducing yield. In this example, plants from a natural population originating from a volcanic island in Cape Verde, Fogo, developed chlorosis when grown in standard potting mix, despite appearing healthy in their native environment.

To investigate the chlorosis observed under greenhouse conditions, I developed an image-based phenotyping approach to quantify chlorosis across thousands of samples.

Ionomics

Characterizing the plant elemental composition

To investigate the nutrient imbalances associated with leaf chlorosis, I used inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS) to characterize leaf elemental composition. This analysis revealed that the Fogo population (orange) showed a distinct leaf ionomic profile compared to their closest relative populations Santo Antão (blue) and Morocco (green).

More broadly, I have used ICP-MS to characterize natural variation in plant elemental composition in global populations, analyzing thousands of samples.

Genetic mapping

Dissecting the genetic basis of ionomic variation

To investigate the genetic basis of leaf chlorosis and ionomic variation, I have applied quantitative genetic approaches, including genome-wide association studies (GWAS) and quantitative trait locus (QTL) mapping in natural and experimental populations.

In this example, I used bulk-segregant analysis (BSA) to identify natural genetic variation at two nutrient transporter genes, IRT1 and NRAMP1, associated with variation in leaf chlorosis.

Environmental association analysis

Identifying environmental predictors of ionomic variation

In this example, leaf magnesium (Mg) content exhibited a latitudinal cline across natural populations.

I used machine learning approaches to identify environmental factors associated with this latitudinal variation in leaf Mg content.
