Functional molecular genetics

From candidate variants to biological function

I combine molecular and functional genetic approaches to characterize candidate variants and understand how they influence gene function and plant phenotypes. My work includes CRISPR/Cas9 genome editing, RNA-seq and allele-specific expression analyses, eQTL mapping, and the characterization of complex structural variants, particularly tandem duplications and gene copy number variation.

Example 1

Genome editing

A natural population, Fogo, exhibited a distinct ionomic profile relative to their closest relative populations Santo Antão and Morocco. Using bulk-segregant analysis (BSA), I identified a loss-of-function variant in IRT1, a gene encoding a divalent cation transporter essential for iron homeostasis.

Using CRISPR-Cas in a closely related population, Santo Antão, I demonstrated that loss of IRT1 recapitulated the ionomic shift observed in the Fogo population.

Example 2

Gene copy number variation

Here, bulk-segregant analysis (BSA) identified an association between copy number variation at NRAMP1, a gene encoding a nutrient transporter, and leaf chlorosis. Further investigation of the natural population from which the parental accession carrying the additional NRAMP1 copy originated revealed extensive copy number variation at this locus. Remarkably, NRAMP1 copy number estimates based on short-read sequencing data ranged from one to six copies, despite the low genetic diversity of this population.

I confirmed NRAMP1 copy number variation by analyzing short- and long-read sequencing data, and by using a probe-based digital PCR assay.
